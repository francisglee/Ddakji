## Summary

Institutional AI strategy (2026-07-09) for legal education; generalizes
well beyond it. Core distinction: AI uses that "increase students'
effort and engagement" (clarifying background, generating practice)
versus shortcuts that "produce easy answers but stunt intellectual
growth" — the risk named as **cognitive offloading**, avoiding the
effortful struggle that develops skill. Strategy: "AI-resilient
pedagogy" rather than bans — build the skill *without* AI as the
foundation, then layer in supervised AI use, so practitioners can both
work independently and "supervise AI and critique its output."
Verification happens where technology can't be leaned on (in-person
exams, oral defenses). Three pillars: AI-resilient practice, elevated
human skills (judgment, advocacy, relationships), responsible AI use.

## What it argues

Skill develops through effortful engagement; AI should raise that
effort, not replace it; competence with AI is a *supervisory* skill
built on top of independent ability, not a substitute for it.
