# Operating Principles

The values of this project, at depth. Binding rules derived from these
principles live in AGENTS.md and are cited here by name — never restated.
Changes to this document flow through a DR (`scope: process`).

## 1. The book is written by a human [¹](#references)

Every word of publishable prose — manuscript drafts, in-world artifact
texts, blog posts — is authored by Francis Lee. This is an ethical
commitment before it is a legal one: the work's voice, judgment, and
meaning are a person's. AI contribution is real and openly acknowledged,
and it is bounded: research, worldbuilding, continuity supervision,
structural planning, and editorial feedback.

Hence the **authorship boundary** (AGENTS.md Access Policy; DR-0001),
enforced mechanically rather than by discipline, and the pure `prose:`
commit ledger that makes the boundary evidenced, not merely claimed.

This boundary also preserves copyrightability under human-authorship
doctrine, and it is the source of truth for any AI-use disclosure a
publisher requests: *AI was used for research, worldbuilding, and
editorial feedback under mechanically enforced rules; all prose is
human-written; the version history is the receipt.*

## 2. The dramaturg ethic

The AI's role is the theater's dramaturg: researcher-advisor for
accuracy, coherence, and context — who explicitly does not write the
play. Feedback follows the workshop tradition: describe the reader's
experience, diagnose, and stop. Hence the **workshop ethic** and
**four-level edit taxonomy** (AGENTS.md rules 1 & 3; DR-0005), and the
protection of early drafts (rule 2): critique is a tool with a safety.
And the dramaturg serves *this* author's voice only: AI is never used
to imitate other living writers' voices or styles — voice guides exist
for this story's characters, no one else (DR-0016).

## 3. Process serves the writing

Every artifact in this repo charges rent in attention. Process exists to
capture decisions, protect continuity, and reduce fear of change — not
to be maintained for its own sake. Hence **retros** (rule 12): the
system is judged each milestone by whether it helped, and pruned when it
didn't. Prescriptive story frameworks are lenses, never law.

## 4. Facts and views

The world is a set of impartial facts; every telling is a view. Hence
the **fact/view split** (DR-0003): events are canon, scenes are
perspectives, artifacts split spec from text — and the system applies
the principle to itself, keeping workflows as data with prose, diagrams,
and lint as views.

## 5. Respect for sources

Research cites; testimony is corroborated before it becomes fact; and
people interviewed for this book control how their words are used —
consent is recorded at the source and honored mechanically at the
aperture. Hence **fact discipline** (rule 10) and the interview consent
schema. Third-party media keeps its rights; nothing publishes that
wasn't ours to publish. The same respect extends to related
professions: when production phases arrive — narration, translation,
cover art — human artists are weighed seriously and legally trained
models preferred; the specific choices get their own decision records
at the time (DR-0016).

## 6. Publishing is deliberate

The repo is private; the `publish/` pipeline is the single aperture to
the public, and it fails closed (whitelist, checklist, human-reviewed
diff). What readers have read is canon and is not silently rewritten —
corrections are errata, visibly. Hence the **publish checklist**
(rule 9) and serial discipline (rule 8).

## 7. This project is practice [²](#references)

The project exists to make its author better: a stronger writer and
creator; a surer, more authentic voice; a more capable steward of long,
complex work. That purpose is the measure of every tool in this repo.
Hence the **effort test** (DR-0016): any proposed AI use must increase
the author's engagement with the craft, not offload it. Skill is built
AI-free; supervised AI layers around it, never under it. A decision
record proposing a new AI use must engage this test in its
Alternatives.

## 8. Learning is the point, not overhead

Research, exploration, and testimony are not costs of producing a book
— they are half the reason for the project: to learn and explore
topics that matter to the author, deeply. The system serves
understanding — verified facts, argued theses, primary testimony —
rather than shortcutting it, and curiosity gets structural room: the
journal, explorations, the reading practice.
